# Welcome

**Welcome to Fewer.**

Fewer is a focused work search. Instead of blasting applications, you pick a small set of companies where you’d do genuinely good work, find warm paths to people who can hire you, and build real conversations.

**What sets it apart**

- **We don’t lead with applications.** Your resume is a closing document, not an opening one.

## What Fewer is

Fewer is a structured way to run a full-time job search. The method is simple: get clear on what you’re after, pick a small set of companies worth your energy, and work your network toward the people who can actually hire you.

It is not a job board, an auto-apply tool, or a resume optimizer. It’s built for discipline: fewer companies, deeper work, better conversations.

---

## Why applying doesn’t work

It’s become a trope: apply to hundreds of roles, get a handful of screens, land nothing. Both sides of hiring are broken for the same reason: volume.

As a hiring manager, the pile is overwhelming. I was once receiving hundreds of applications a week, and that was before AI made bulk applying even easier for roles that aren’t even a good fit. There’s simply no good way to manually review that much noise. ATS tools exist because humans can’t keep up. Usually only a small slice is worth considering, and knowing which slice is the hard part.

As a candidate, it sucks. You’re a row in a database, hoping an algorithm picks you out of thousands of nearly identical applications. It makes you feel powerless.

What can you do, then?

Stop applying. Really. Don’t even bother until you’re already in an interview process with a hiring manager.

**Your resume is a closing document, not an opening one.** Send it when a hiring manager asks for it. Don’t lead with it and hope someone else invents a next step for you.

Contingency recruiters optimize for placements, not for you. Internal recruiters can help you navigate a process, but they usually can’t hire you. The activities that feel most like “looking for a job” are often the ones least likely to get you in front of the one person who matters.

---

## The one person who matters

**The hiring manager is the only person who matters.**

They decide whether a role exists, what it looks like, who fits, and whether you get an offer. Committees and processes exist, but without being in front of that person, you don’t get hired. That isn’t a clever strategy. It’s a logical fact.

So the whole method is one sentence: **get to the person who has the power to hire you.** Everything in Fewer exists to make that reliable.

---

## Aim for Good Work

“Good Work” means work that energizes and sustains you, not just a logo or a paycheck that looks right on paper. Contentment beats happiness as a target. Every job has challenges. The point is figuring out which challenges energize you and which ones drain you.

Without clarity, you’ll either get rejected everywhere or accept the wrong seat and learn the hard way. Fewer’s early phases put that clarity into writing before you spend weeks on the wrong list.

You don’t need external reading or assessments to start. Phase 1 interviews you into a short profile and voice, and Phase 2 turns that into search criteria.

---

## The method

1. **Get clear.** Best work, edge, drains, non-negotiables. Know what you’re for and what you’re not for.
2. **Build a tight target list.** Companies you’d actually be excited to join, even if they aren’t hiring. Depth over breadth. A list of fifty is spray-and-pray with better branding.
3. **Find warm paths.** Someone in your network who knows someone there. First-degree is best, second-degree intros work, and recently departed people often tell the truth.
4. **Low-pressure outreach.** Ask to learn about their experience, and make it clear that even though you’re in the market, you don’t expect them to have a role for you. That removes pressure. You’re not asking for a resume forward or an invented opening.
5. **Have real conversations.** These are exploratory. You’re trying to learn what it’s actually like to work there, whether the challenges match the ones that engage you, and whether how they work matches how you like to work. That kind of signal doesn’t show up on a company webpage. They’re also getting a read on you: chemistry, fit, whether there’s something there.
6. **Pull the thread.** Each conversation can lead to the next name, closer to the hiring manager. When someone gives you a name, reach out within a couple of days, always say who referred you, and keep the outreach low-pressure even as you get closer.
7. **Talk to the hiring manager.** If there’s no role, stay informational. If they like you, they may create one. If there *is* a role, the chat becomes evaluative, and *then* resume and process can matter, because you’re no longer a random applicant.
8. **Keep the pipeline alive.** Don’t bet everything on one thread. Follow up simply: ping, wait about a week, ping, wait about two weeks, one more, then stop. Track **hiring-manager conversations**, not applications sent.

The target list is living. Companies fall off when you learn they’re wrong, and new ones appear because conversations pointed you there. Hold it firmly enough to focus, and loosely enough to learn.

This is how friends and I have been finding roles for years: low-volume, targeted paths that lead to genuine conversations. Not the ATS lottery.

## Why Fewer feels strict

**Fewer, better.** Most tools remove friction from applying. Fewer adds friction to *choosing*, and that’s intentional.

**Quality of choosing over throughput.** This is not “see more jobs.” It’s “decide which companies earn your energy, then work them properly.”

**Active-conversation cap (default 5).** Only a small number of companies can be in active, post-outreach conversation at once. The cap creates focus, reduces desperation, and forces a refill decision when something ends.

**Bullets before cannons.** As soon as one company is worth pursuing, move to people and outreach. Don’t wait to finish rating a spreadsheet before you talk to a human.

**Judgment stays yours.** The system helps with structure, criteria, scoring, drafts, and follow-ups. You rate companies, choose people, send messages, and decide what’s a fit.

**One metric that matters:** hiring-manager conversations.

## What this feels like

The process works when you work it, and it’s still emotionally hard.

Good weeks create false “I’m almost done” feelings. Quiet weeks feel like failure. Neither is a full signal, so keep prospecting, and don’t stop the funnel when one thread looks hot.

Scarcity thinking (every conversation is your last chance) makes you worse in the room. A small active pipeline is how you stay abundant without spraying the market.

Unlike the heavier “treat the search like a full-time job” framing, this process is meant to shrink the time commitment. When I used it to find my own job, it worked as a daily habit of about 30 minutes. Take care of the rest of your life. You don’t have to do this alone. Use this chat as a second brain for structure, drafts, and next steps.
